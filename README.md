        <h3>Chemical Bonding (Covalent Bonding)</h3>
    </header>

    <h2>Sharing Electrons</h2>
    <p>Some atoms form <strong>ionic bonds</strong> between oppositely charged ions.</p>
    
    <div class="note-box">
        <strong>Example: Sodium Chloride [Na]<sup>+</sup> [Cl]<sup>-</sup></strong><br>
        They transfer electrons to get a full outer shell. They are attracted through electrostatic forces which hold them together.
    </div>

    <h3>Displayed Formula</h3>
    <p>Shows how atoms are linked. For example, a chlorine molecule is drawn as:</p>
    <div class="formula-box">Cl — Cl</div>
    
    <p>This notation is used for big molecules that would otherwise be too complicated to draw entirely. As seen in:</p>
    <strong>SUGAR (Molecular Structure Example):</strong>
    <div class="formula-box">
     C
     |
   H-C—O—H
    /   \
   C     C
  /       \
HO-C—————C-OH
    </div>

    <div class="highlight-title">What about two non-metal elements?</div>
    <p>Instead of transferring, they <strong>share electrons</strong> so that both can achieve a full outer shell.</p>

    <h3>Dot & Cross Diagram (Chlorine Molecule - Cl<sub>2</sub>)</h3>
    <ul>
        <li>Both chlorine atoms need 1 extra electron.</li>
        <li>They overlap their outer shells to share a pair of electrons.</li>
        <li><strong>Result:</strong> Two electrons are being shared in the center intersection (each atom gets 1 extra effectively).</li>
    </ul>

    <hr style="border: 0; border-top: 1px solid #ddd; margin: 30px 0;">

    <h2>Covalent Bonding in Ammonia (NH<sub>3</sub>)</h2>
    
    <h3>Outermost Shell Configurations:</h3>
    <ul>
        <li><strong>Hydrogen (H):</strong> First energy level. Maximum 2 electrons (needs 1 extra).</li>
        <li><strong>Nitrogen (N):</strong> Second energy level. Maximum 8 electrons (needs 3 extras).</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>The Molecule Setup:</h3>
    <p>One Nitrogen atom sits in the center sharing its 3 valence electrons individually with 3 separate Hydrogen atoms. Each intersection contains a dot-and-cross electron pair.</p>

    <h3>Displayed Formula for Ammonia:</h3>
    <p>Place a line wherever there was a covalent bond:</p>
    <div class="formula-box">
  H — N — H
      |
      H
    </div>

    <h2>Types of Substances Covalent Bonds Can Make</h2>
    
    <div class="grid-split">
        <div class="column">
            <h3>Simple Molecular Substances</h3>
            <p>Consist of small molecules with strong internal covalent bonds.</p>
            <p><strong>Examples:</strong></p>
            <ul>
                <li>Ammonia (NH<sub>3</sub>)</li>
                <li>Chlorine gas (Cl — Cl)</li>
            </ul>
        </div>
        
        <div class="column">
            <h3>Larger Structures</h3>
            
            <h4>Polymers:</h4>
            <p>Form long chains consisting of repeating units (monomers).</p>
            <div class="formula-box">O—O—O—O—O—O—O</div>
            
            <h4>Giant Covalent Structures:</h4>
            <ul>
                <li>Silicon dioxide</li>
                <li>Diamond</li>
                <li>Graphite</li>
            </ul>
            <p><em>Note: Since they are completely joined by covalent bonds throughout, they are really strong structures!</em></p>
        </div>
